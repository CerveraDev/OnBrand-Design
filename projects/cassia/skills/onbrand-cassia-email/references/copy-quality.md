# Copy Quality

Apply this reference after drafting campaign copy and before user approval or HTML assembly. The goal is specific, credible, project-authentic writing. Do not claim that copy is human-authored, undetectable, or guaranteed to pass an AI detector.

## Decision Order

When guidance conflicts, use this order:

1. Factual accuracy, supplied legal language, and compliance constraints
2. Approved Cassia brand voice and terminology
3. Campaign objective, audience, and CTA
4. Clarity and natural voice
5. Pattern cleanup

Do not remove an approved project term merely because it appears on a generic AI-writing watchlist.

## Silent Editorial Pass

Run this pass on copy created by the email skill:

- Identify the one thing the reader should understand or do.
- Apply the portability test: replace lines that could describe any luxury development with a supported Cassia detail, consequence, or point of view.
- Replace ornamental claims with concrete facts from approved inputs.
- Remove throat-clearing, fake insight, importance puffery, repetitive summaries, and commentary that tells the reader what to think.
- Check for repeated binary contrasts, automatic groups of three, stacked fragments, uniform sentence shapes, and decorative punctuation. Revise only when the pattern makes the copy feel canned.
- Prefer direct verbs and active construction when they improve clarity. Do not force active voice when approved legal language or meaning calls for another construction.
- Keep terminology consistent instead of rotating synonyms for variety.
- Preserve useful cadence, restraint, and distinctive project language. Make the minimum effective edit.
- Verify every date, number, availability statement, price, award, quotation, testimonial, and superlative against supplied material. Remove or flag unsupported claims.
- Flag fair-housing, financial, legal, or other compliance-sensitive language for human review rather than asserting compliance.

## Words To Inspect, Not Ban

Words such as `vibrant`, `stunning`, `seamless`, `elevated`, `transformative`, `iconic`, `exclusive`, and `unparalleled` often carry little information. Keep one only when it is approved brand language or supported by a specific detail. Replace it when it is decorative, repeated, or interchangeable with another property.

Treat the same way any phrase that announces importance without demonstrating it, including generic references to lifestyle, sophistication, innovation, craftsmanship, or a pivotal moment.

## Email-Specific Checks

- Subject line makes a specific promise without clickbait or unsupported urgency.
- Preview text extends the subject line instead of repeating it.
- Hero headline carries the campaign idea without restating the subject word for word.
- Body copy is short enough to scan on a phone and contains only useful support.
- CTA states the intended action and matches the destination supplied by the user.
- Variants keep the same approved campaign meaning unless the user requests different copy.

## Audit Mode

When the user asks to audit copy without rewriting it, report each material issue with:

- The quoted passage
- The pattern or risk
- Why it weakens this campaign
- A brief correction direction

Do not score the probability that AI wrote the text. Do not rewrite the full draft unless the user asks.

## Final Read

Read the finished copy once for meaning and once aloud for rhythm. Remove editing notes and internal process language from campaign output. If cleanup makes the copy less accurate, less distinctive, or less like the approved brand, restore the stronger version.
